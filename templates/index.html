Clinical Pathology Center

Review Diabetic Retinopathy pathology classes, symptoms, and indicators

What is Diabetic Retinopathy (DR)?

Diabetic Retinopathy is a severe ocular pathology resulting from chronic hyperglycemia in patients with Diabetes Mellitus. It causes microvascular damage to the light-sensitive retinal layer at the posterior segment of the eye. Capitulated leaks, exudate swelling, and ischemia of retinal tissues trigger vision deterioration, hemorrhage, and potential blindness.

Grade 0: Normal Retina

Pathology: No microvascular lesions. Captain vessel networks appear continuous and crisp.
AI Classification: Non-Referable DR.
Action Protocol: Dilated retinal photography screening on an annual cycle.

Grade 1-2: NPDR

Pathology: Captures microaneurysms (dilated capillary tips), blot/dot hemorrhages, and lipid hard exudates.
AI Classification: Referable DR (Moderate).
Action Protocol: Direct clinical referral. Ophthalmology review within 3 months.

Grade 3-4: Severe / PDR

Pathology: Extensive capillary closure (cotton wool spots), preretinal hemorrhages, and neovascularization on optic disc.
AI Classification: Referable DR (Proliferative).
Action Protocol: Critical emergency referral. Immediate panretinal photocoagulation (PRP) review.
